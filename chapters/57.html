Tafsir of the Surah Section 1 of Chapter 57 [Verse 1-6]: Presence of Allah as Hidden and Evident Entity

He is the First and the Last, the Evident and the Hidden, and He has full knowledge of all things. Remarks:

Necessary Background Knowledge: Chapter-1 (Tafsir of Surah Fatihah).

The last paragraph of the above verses is confusing. 'The First and the Last' means 'the Beginning and the End.' Allah has no beginning and no end. So, how is He the First and the Last? A similar verse is also found in the Holy Bible:

"I am the Alpha and the Omega, the First and the Last, the Beginning and the End." – Revelation 22:13 Holy Bible

The verse in the Holy Bible may escape scrutiny, as the Bible is very old and has been distorted. But what about the Quran? Allah cannot be the First and the Last in any case. Actually, the verse in the Quran is different from the one in the Holy Bible. While they may appear similar, they do not carry the same meaning. The middle part of the Quranic verse describes Allah as both Evident (Jahir) and Hidden (Batin). Therefore, the overall verse conveys a different meaning than the obvious one. This is discussed further below: ―He is the First and the Last…‖ means that Allah encompasses the entire creation, from the beginning to the end (the creation has a beginning and an end, but Allah does not). Allah is ―…the Evident and the Hidden…‖ meaning that He encompasses creation from the deepest to the highest levels. He is active at the level of fundamental subatomic particles, where scientists perceive some of His acts as the acts of Quantum Force Fields. He is also active at the level of stars, galaxies, and the universe, with scientists interpreting some of His acts as the acts of Gravitational Force. Thus, the last part of the verse ‗…He has full knowledge of all things‘ is justified. One may notice that the terms 'Evident (Jahir)' and 'Hidden (Batin)' do not appear in the verse of the Holy Bible. Instead, it repeats words of similar meaning three times: 'Alpha and Omega', 'the First and the Last', and 'the Beginning and the End.' Therefore, the verse of the Holy Bible does not convey God's all-embracing presence in the creation, which would imply the possession of full knowledge of everything. It is likely that the verse in the Holy Bible was mistranslated in ancient times.
