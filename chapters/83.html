Tafsir of the Surah Section 1 of Chapter 83 [Verse 1-6]: Fraud

Woe to those that deal in fraud! Those who when they have to receive by measure from men exact full measure, but when they have to give by measure or weight to men give less than due. Do they not think that they will be called to account on a Mighty Day, a Day when mankind will stand before the Lord of the universes? Section 2 of Chapter 83 [Verse 7-9]: Sijjin

Nay! Surely the book of the wicked is in Sijjin. And what will explain to thee what Sijjin is! A book inscribed!

Remarks:

The Sijjin and Illiyin are virtual worlds. The deceased will live their virtual lives in these worlds until the Day of Resurrection. Sijjin is for those heading to Hell, and Illiyin is for those heading to Jannah. I have discussed the Sijjin in the sequence below: 1. What is Sijjin? 2. Moving into the Sijjin?

1. What is Sijjin?

The above verses state that Sijjin is an inscribed book: ―And what will explain to thee what Sijjin is! A book inscribed!‖ With the words, ―And what will explain to thee what Sijjin is!‖ the verses emphasize that it is not just a simple book; there is something deeper to understand within it. In Section-9 of Chapter-6, we discussed how a Pen wrote our fates in the Saved Disc (Lawh-Mahfuz). The following verses indicate that a Motherboard is also involved with both the Pen and the Disc:

―By the book that makes things clear, We have made it a Qur'an in Arabic that ye may be able to understand. And verily, it is in the ‗Mother of the Book‘ in Our presence, high, full of wisdom.‖ [Al Quran 43: 2-4]

The 'Mother of the Book' can give birth to a book. Therefore, it is a highly developed Motherboard that controls both the Pen and the Disc. The Pen, the Disc, and the Mother Board collectively form a computer. I refer to it as the 'Central Computer of the Universes' or 'CCU' for short. The Quran was written by the CCU; however, Allah edited it, as indicated by the following verse:

―Allah does blot out (delete) or confirm (save) what He pleases: with Him is the Mother of the Book.‖ [Al Quran 13:39]

The Quran refers to a file of the CCU as a 'book'. Therefore, the file of the Quran, which is preserved in the CCU, is called a 'book'. Similarly, Sijjin is referred to as a 'book,' as the verses say: 'A book inscribed!' Therefore, Sijjin may be a file of the CCU. However, based on its activities, it seems more likely to be a folder or possibly an additional hard disk of the CCU. It is a 'book inscribed,' which means that the file, folder, or hard disk is programmed to perform specific tasks. Therefore, Sijjin is a 'book' (file, folder, or hard disk) of the CCU where the Records of Deeds are stored. On the other hand, we know from the Hadith that the souls (nafses) of sinful deceased persons are stored in Sijjin, which is located in the deep Barzakh. Therefore, Sijjin has two main parts, as follows:  Part-1 is in a file / folder / additional hard disc of the CCU. This part may also be a server computer within the network of CCU, where the Records of Deeds are stored.  Part 2 is a domain in the deep Barzakh where the souls (nafses) of the sinners are stored. This domain is the virtual world of the dead sinners, managed by the CCU or the assisting server computer.

2. Moving into the Sijjin

After the death of a person, his soul (nafs) is taken to Allah. According to the following verses, it converses with Allah:

―Say: "The Angel of Death, put in charge of you, will take your souls; then shall ye be brought back to your Lord." If only thou could see when the guilty ones will bend low their heads before their Lord, "Our Lord! We have seen, and we have heard. Now then send us back; we will work righteousness; for we do indeed (now) believe."" [Al Quran 32:11-12]

But the soul (nafs) is a combination of unknown force fields. While it may contain vast amounts of imprinted information, it is unlikely to have the capacity to interact with Allah directly. Therefore, the nafs is provided with a virtual form, and supplied with memory data. As discussed in Section-9 of Chapter-6, at first Allah created a virtual universe in the CCU as His Master Design. Adam and Eve, and subsequently each human, were created in this virtual universe from digital double helix DNA molecules.

"When thy Lord drew forth from the children of Adam—from their back—their descendants, and made them testify concerning themselves (saying): "Am I not your Lord?" They said: "Yea! We do testify!..." [Al Quran 7:172]

Thus, the CCU contains the genome code for each human, and it can produce a virtual version of him. But a digital human cannot possess emotions, so his nafs is entangled with the virtual form. It also requires memory data, which is provided by the CCU. According to the Quran, the brain data of each human is collected every night by the angel and deposited in the CCU:

―It is He who kills you (yatawaffakum) by night, and He knows what you committed by the day. Then He raises you up therein so that term specified is fulfilled. In the end, unto Him will be your return. Then He will show you what you used to do.‖ [Al Quran 6:60]

With the genome code, nafs, and memory data, a human is virtually created for the afterlife and presented before God through holographic projection from the CCU or a dedicated Server Computer when he says: "Our Lord! We have seen, and we have heard. Now then send us back; we will work righteousness; for we do indeed (now) believe." Allah decides his final destination, placing him in either Illiyin or Sijjin. The CCU or its server computer projects the human into a holographic world where other deceased humans are already living their virtual lives. The CCU has the necessary software and systems to maintain a person almost like a living human. The individual can answer questions based on their past life. The virtual people can converse with each other.

4. Nafs develops in the Sijjin

The nafs develops in the mother's womb alongside the body. As composite force fields, the nafs becomes imprinted with the information of the development. It is designed and programmed to support the growth. At the time of death, the nafs becomes fixed. The nafs is composed of force fields. Once it becomes fixed, it requires extreme heat for modification. For example, a temperature of 1028 degree Kelvin is needed to merge the Strong Nuclear Force Field with the Electromagnetic Force Field. Such a temperature could be achieved if one were able to compress the entire universe into the state of the Big Bang. The fundamental nature of the nafs does not change after death, but it can grow tougher, larger, and stronger. In the Sijjin, the nafs of a pagan undergoes significant development in terms of size, strength, endurance, and other attributes. In the Sijjin, the nafs exists without a material body, and as a result, it develops a heightened sense and drive for survival. The nafs is also punished by the extreme fire of Hell to make necessary corrections, ensuring it becomes perfectly fit to endure the violence of Hell. The Sijjin does not develop the nafs to prepare a person for the Jannaat; rather, it develops it to make him fit for Hell. A soul from the Sijjin will resurrect a human in the form of a devil-human, as a multi-dimensional being thousand kilometers tall. According to the Hadith, he will require the space from Makkah to Madinah just to sit, and his teeth will be as large as the Mount of Uhud. The memories of the virtual life are not added to a person‘s memory record, nor are they recorded in the Amal-Nama. Therefore, when a person is resurrected, he will forget his virtual life and will feel as though one day he died on Earth, and the next day he is resurrected, even though millions of years may have passed in extreme punishments. Before coming to Earth, we had a virtual life in the Master Design as well (in the CCU), where we acknowledged that Allah was our Lord. However, we have since forgotten it. The Sijjin is located in the depths of Barzakh. The void of Barzakh does not allow a nafs to escape.

Section 3 of Chapter 83 [Verse10-17]: Stain has covered over their Qalbs

Woe that Day to those that deny—those that deny the Day of Judgment, and none can deny it but the transgressor beyond bounds, the sinner! When Our verses are rehearsed to him, he says, "Tales of the ancients!" By no means! But stain has covered over their qalbs what they used to earn. No! Indeed, from their Lord that Day they will be partitioned. Further, they will enter the Fire of Hell. Further, it will be said to them, ―This is that ye rejected as false!‖

Remarks:

The qalb (mind) is located in the chest. It can be described as a virtual brain, produced by a special ruh, along with several muscles of the chest, nerves, and the brain itself. The qalb is discussed in detail in Section-10 of Chapter-6. As the above verses indicate, the qalbs can become stained due to what they used to earn, including the alterations cased due to the mounted satanic jinns. Therefore, a mounted jinni stains and deforms the nafs of an idolater. A deformed or stained nafs produces erroneous emotions in the qalb (mind) and drives the individual down the wrong path. The harm to the qalb is discussed here because the virtual punishments of Illiyin and Sijjin are meant to purify and develop the nafs.

Section-4 of Chapter 83 [Verse18-21]: Illiyin

Nay, verily the Book of the Righteous is in 'Illiyin. And what will explain to thee what 'Illiyin is? A Book inscribed, to which bear witness those Nearest. Remarks:

Illiyin is an establishment similar to Sijjin, but it is reserved for those destined for Jannaat. In Illiyin, the nafs (soul) is purified and developed so that the person becomes fit for Jannaat. Some nafses are punished for their earthly sins, allowing them to be resurrected as sinless humans. This is known as the punishment of the grave (Azab al-Qabr). It is possible to punish a person in virtual reality because the human brain cannot differentiate between virtual reality and physical reality. For example, if one imagines sitting on the edge of a high rooftop, one will experience fear. Similarly, real-life pain is also a form of virtual pain, as it is perceived through the signals processed by the brain. The Araf is located at the highest level of Barzakh, and it is likely that Illiyin is situated just below it. Sijjin, on the other hand, is found at the deepest level of Barzakh. The zooming power of Illiyin can extend beyond its holographic realm when aided by Sidratul-Muntaha. As a result, the nafses residing in Illiyin can visit the Earth, often appearing to their relatives at the time of death. This is why, at the moment of death, a Muslim may see his deceased relatives coming to receive him. This does not occur for a pagan. No nafs can come from the holographic realm of Sijjin to welcome someone heading towards it. The nafses in Sijjin are formidable giants, and allowing them to leave would be too risky. [At the time of death, if a person says that such and such deceased relatives have come to receive him, it indicates that he is heading towards Illiyin (and thus to Jannaat). This is a very good sign, one that occurs with almost every Muslim].

Section 5 of Chapter 83 [Verse 22-36]: On Thrones will they Command a Sight

Truly, the Righteous will be in Bliss; on thrones will they command a sight. Thou will recognize in their faces the beaming brightness of Bliss. Their thirst will be slaked with pure wine sealed. The seal thereof will be musk. And for this let those aspire who have aspirations. With it will be a mixture of Tasnim, a spring from whereof drink those Nearest to God. Those in sin used to laugh at those who believed, and whenever they passed by them used to wink at each other. And when they returned to their own people, they would return jesting. And whenever they saw them, they would say, "Behold! These are the people truly astray!" But they had not been sent as keepers over them! But on this day the Believers will laugh at the Unbelievers—on thrones they will command; will not the Unbelievers have been paid back for what they did?

Remarks:

In the first paragraph of the above verses, it says, ―On thrones will they command a sight‖ and in the last paragraph, ―But on this day the Believers will laugh at the Unbelievers—on thrones they will command...‖ These points require discussion. The verses are referring to the throne upon which a person in Jannaat will command a sight: ―On thrones will they command a sight‖. This sight is not of the surrounding area, but of Hell, where the Unbelievers will reside. The Jannaat is a separate universe altogether, even larger than this universe (Samawaat). On the other hand, we know from the Quran and Hadith that the objects of Hell are created within this universe (Samawaat). Therefore, it should not be possible for someone in Jannaat to observe those in the Samawaat without a system in place. It seems that the people of Jannaat will have something akin to the Internet. However, they will not need to operate a personal computer. Instead, they will have thrones from which they can command view. If a person wishes to observe someone in Hell, a 3D view will instantly appear before him. The connection will be so advanced that he will be able to see and speak with the one in Hell without any delay or buffering. In our understanding, such communication might be possible through a teleportation technique. However, there is knowledge beyond our comprehension, and there may be other ways to achieve this as well. The connection may be rooted through Sidratul- Muntaha, which is the heart of a vast cybernetic system encompassing the universes. This system is discussed in Section-9 of Chapter-6.
